**Q1.** An organization is implementing a security program for the first time. Which framework provides the broadest governance structure for aligning security with business objectives?

A) NIST SP 800-53
B) ISO/IEC 27001
C) COBIT
D) ITIL

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C) COBIT**

COBIT (Control Objectives for Information and Related Technologies) is primarily a governance framework that aligns IT (including security) with business objectives. ISO/IEC 27001 is an information security management standard. NIST SP 800-53 is a catalog of security controls. ITIL focuses on IT service management. When the question emphasizes governance and business alignment, COBIT is the answer.

</details>

**Q2.** A CISSP candidate is reviewing a contract with a cloud provider. The contract states the provider will notify the client within 72 hours of discovering a breach. This is an example of which concept?

A) Service level agreement
B) Data processing agreement
C) Memorandum of understanding
D) Interconnection security agreement

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B) Data processing agreement**

A data processing agreement (DPA) governs how a vendor processes personal data on behalf of a client, including breach notification timelines. This is required under regulations like GDPR. An SLA defines performance metrics. An MOU is a non-binding statement of intent. An ISA defines technical and security requirements for system interconnections.

</details>

**Q3.** Which risk management approach involves eliminating the risk entirely by discontinuing the activity that introduces it?

A) Risk transference
B) Risk mitigation
C) Risk acceptance
D) Risk avoidance

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D) Risk avoidance**

Risk avoidance eliminates the risk by not engaging in the risky activity. Risk transference shifts financial responsibility to a third party (e.g., insurance). Risk mitigation reduces the likelihood or impact through controls. Risk acceptance acknowledges the risk and proceeds without additional controls, often when cost of mitigation exceeds potential loss.

</details>

**Q4.** An annual loss expectancy (ALE) calculation results in $50,000 for a specific threat. A countermeasure costing $60,000 per year would reduce the ALE to $5,000. What should the security manager recommend?

A) Implement the countermeasure because it reduces risk by 90%
B) Do not implement the countermeasure because it is not cost-effective
C) Implement the countermeasure because ALE exceeds $50,000
D) Accept the risk and document the decision

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B) Do not implement the countermeasure because it is not cost-effective**

Cost-benefit analysis: the countermeasure costs $60,000/year but only saves $45,000/year ($50,000 - $5,000 residual ALE). Net loss of $15,000/year makes it economically unjustifiable. The correct recommendation is to either find a less expensive control or accept the risk. A 90% reduction sounds impressive, but cost-effectiveness is the determining factor.

</details>

**Q5.** Which of the following BEST describes the purpose of a business impact analysis (BIA)?

A) Identifying all possible threats to an organization
B) Determining the maximum tolerable downtime for critical business functions
C) Calculating the return on investment for security controls
D) Documenting current security policies and procedures

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B) Determining the maximum tolerable downtime for critical business functions**

A BIA identifies critical business functions and determines their recovery time objectives (RTO), recovery point objectives (RPO), and maximum tolerable downtime (MTD). It prioritizes which systems and processes must be restored first after a disruption. Threat identification is part of risk assessment. ROI calculations are cost-benefit analysis. Policy documentation is a separate activity.

</details>

**Q6.** A security professional is drafting an organization's information security policy. At what level should this document exist?

A) Operational level, defining specific technical procedures
B) Tactical level, guiding project-specific security decisions
C) Strategic level, expressing management's security intent and direction
D) Technical level, specifying configuration standards

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C) Strategic level, expressing management's security intent and direction**

Security policies exist at the strategic level and express senior management's commitment and intent. They are high-level, non-technical documents. Procedures are operational-level documents specifying how to implement controls. Standards and baselines are tactical. Configuration guides are technical. The policy hierarchy flows: Policy → Standard → Guideline → Procedure.

</details>

C) Protect society, the common good, necessary public trust and confidence, and the infrastructure

**Q8.** Which legal concept holds that executives can be held personally liable for the security failures of their organization if they failed to exercise reasonable oversight?

A) Prudent person rule
B) Due diligence
C) Downstream liability
D) Respondeat superior

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A) Prudent person rule**

The prudent person rule (also called the reasonable person standard) holds that executives must exercise the same level of care a reasonable, prudent person would in their position. Failure to do so can result in personal liability. Due diligence is the process of exercising that care. Downstream liability refers to liability passed to affected third parties. Respondeat superior is employer liability for employee actions.

</details>

**Q9.** An organization operates in multiple jurisdictions with conflicting privacy laws. Which approach BEST addresses this challenge?

A) Follow the laws of the organization's home country only
B) Apply the most restrictive requirements across all operations
C) Negotiate with regulators to determine applicable law
D) Implement the least common denominator of all applicable laws

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B) Apply the most restrictive requirements across all operations**

When operating under conflicting jurisdictional requirements, applying the most restrictive standard ensures compliance in all jurisdictions. This is especially relevant for privacy regulations like GDPR, CCPA, and others. Following only home-country law risks non-compliance. Negotiating with regulators is impractical. The least common denominator approach would violate stricter jurisdictions.

</details>

**Q10.** What is the PRIMARY difference between a threat agent and a threat?

A) A threat agent is the actual attack; a threat is the potential for attack
B) A threat agent is the entity that exploits a vulnerability; a threat is the potential for harm
C) A threat agent is a natural event; a threat is a human action
D) A threat agent is internal; a threat is external

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B) A threat agent is the entity that exploits a vulnerability; a threat is the potential for harm**

A threat is any potential danger to an asset (e.g., data breach, fire, flood). A threat agent (or threat actor) is the entity that causes the threat to materialize (e.g., hacker, disgruntled employee, hurricane). A vulnerability is the weakness exploited. A risk is the probability that a threat agent will exploit a vulnerability, causing harm.

</details>

**Q11.** A security manager wants to quantify risk using a formula. Which formula correctly represents risk?

A) Risk = Threat × Vulnerability × Asset Value
B) Risk = Threat × Impact
C) Risk = Probability × Impact
D) Risk = Vulnerability / Asset Value

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C) Risk = Probability × Impact**

In the (ISC)² framework, Risk = Probability (likelihood) × Impact (consequence). Some frameworks expand this to include threat and vulnerability components, but the core formula is probability of occurrence multiplied by the resulting impact. This allows quantitative risk scoring and prioritization.

</details>

**Q13.** An organization uses a risk register to track identified risks. What information is MOST important to include for each risk entry?

A) The name of the employee who identified the risk
B) The risk owner, likelihood, impact, and current mitigation status
C) The technical vulnerability details and CVE number
D) The date the risk was discovered and the discovery method

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B) The risk owner, likelihood, impact, and current mitigation status**

A risk register must capture the risk owner (accountable party), likelihood of occurrence, potential impact, and current treatment status (mitigated, accepted, transferred, avoided). This enables tracking and management decisions. The discoverer's name is less important. CVE numbers are too technical for the register. Discovery date has value but is secondary to risk status information.

</details>

**Q15.** A company's security policy requires all employees to complete annual security awareness training. An employee who refuses to complete the training continues to have access to systems. Which control is MISSING?

A) Detective control
B) Preventive control
C) Corrective control
D) Deterrent control

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B) Preventive control**

A preventive control would block system access until training is completed (e.g., account suspension or access revocation). The current situation lacks enforcement — the policy exists but access is not blocked. A detective control would identify non-compliance. A corrective control would remediate after detection. A deterrent discourages non-compliance but doesn't prevent it.

</details>

**Q16.** What does the term "residual risk" mean in risk management?

A) The risk that remains after all controls have been applied
B) The risk that has been transferred to a third party
C) The total risk before any controls are applied
D) The risk that has been formally accepted by management

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A) The risk that remains after all controls have been applied**

Residual risk is the risk remaining after security controls have been implemented. Total (inherent) risk exists before controls. After applying controls, some risk always remains — this is residual risk. Management must formally accept residual risk. Risk that is transferred (e.g., via insurance) shifts financial liability, but the underlying residual risk still exists.

</details>

**Q17.** Which type of law creates obligations between private parties rather than defining crimes against the state?

A) Criminal law
B) Civil law
C) Administrative law
D) Regulatory law

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B) Civil law**

Civil law (also called tort law or private law) governs disputes between private parties and creates obligations such as contracts, negligence, and breach of duty. Criminal law defines crimes against the state. Administrative law governs government agency regulations. Regulatory law is a subset of administrative law. In security, civil law is relevant for breach of contract, negligence claims, and privacy violations.

</details>

**Q19.** Which concept describes the obligation to take action to prevent a harm that you could foresee?

A) Due care
B) Due diligence
C) Duty of loyalty
D) Fiduciary duty

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A) Due care**

Due care is the obligation to act (take steps to prevent foreseeable harm) — it is about doing the right thing. Due diligence is the process of investigating and understanding risks before taking action — it is about knowing the right thing. A common mnemonic: Due Diligence = Did you know? Due Care = Did you act? Both are important for avoiding negligence claims.

</details>

**Q21.** During a risk assessment, the team identifies a vulnerability with a CVSS score of 9.8 but determines the asset it affects has no connection to the internet and is isolated on an air-gapped network. What concept justifies rating this risk lower than the CVSS score implies?

A) Risk transference
B) Compensating controls reducing likelihood
C) Risk appetite threshold
D) Vulnerability scoring normalization

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B) Compensating controls reducing likelihood**

CVSS scores assess technical severity without considering environmental context. The air-gapped network is a compensating control that dramatically reduces the likelihood of exploitation, thus lowering the actual risk rating below what the CVSS score alone suggests. This is why organizations must contextualize vulnerability scores with their specific environment.

</details>

**Q22.** Which term describes the practice of allocating security responsibilities to specific individuals to ensure accountability?

A) Separation of duties
B) Least privilege
C) Job rotation
D) Ownership assignment

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D) Ownership assignment**

Ownership assignment (data/asset ownership) designates a specific individual as responsible and accountable for protecting an asset. Separation of duties divides critical tasks to prevent fraud. Least privilege restricts access to the minimum required. Job rotation periodically moves employees between roles to detect fraud and reduce single-person dependency.

</details>

**Q23.** A security policy states "no employee shall access systems without a valid business need." This policy enforces which foundational security principle?

A) Separation of duties
B) Need to know
C) Least privilege
D) Accountability

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B) Need to know**

Need to know restricts access to information based on whether the individual requires it to perform their job function — even if they have the clearance level, they must have a business need. Least privilege restricts the level of access. Separation of duties divides tasks. Accountability ensures actions can be traced to individuals. Need to know and least privilege work together but are distinct concepts.

</details>

**Q25.** Which risk treatment option is being used when an organization purchases cyber liability insurance?

A) Risk avoidance
B) Risk mitigation
C) Risk transference
D) Risk acceptance

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C) Risk transference**

Purchasing insurance transfers the financial consequences of a risk to the insurer. The risk itself still exists, but the financial impact is shifted. Risk avoidance eliminates the activity causing risk. Risk mitigation reduces likelihood or impact through controls. Risk acceptance acknowledges and documents the risk without additional action.

</details>
